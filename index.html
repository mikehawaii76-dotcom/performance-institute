The Science

Why every other clinic is doing it wrong

Injectable testosterone is the most prescribed treatment in men's health — and the most misunderstood. Understanding the physiology changes everything.

What everyone else prescribes

Injectable Testosterone (TRT)

Synthetic testosterone signals your brain to stop producing the real thing — shutting down your natural testosterone production, sometimes with long-term effects

Testicular shrinkage and infertility — your testes stop working because they're no longer needed

Markedly elevated blood counts (Polycythemia) — a common side effect that thickens the blood and requires regular therapeutic blood donation to manage, increasing stroke and clot risk

Accelerated hair loss and acne from supraphysiologic androgen levels

Weekly injections — forever. Managing needles, syringes, and supplies at home. Monthly trips to the pharmacy to pick up controlled substance prescriptions in person. You cannot stop without a difficult recovery process

The Performance Institute approach

Clomiphene — Natural Optimization

Clomiphene blocks estrogen receptors, tricking your brain into producing more testosterone naturally — your own hormonal system does the work

Fertility preserved and testicular function maintained — or even slightly improved

Dramatically reduced incidence of markedly elevated blood counts (Polycythemia) — a common side effect of injectable testosterone that requires patients to regularly donate blood to manage dangerously thick blood

Dramatically reduced incidence of hair loss compared to injectable testosterone. No injections required — one oral pill taken at home

FAQ

Questions men actually ask

What is the difference between clomiphene and injectable testosterone?

Injectable testosterone suppresses your body's natural testosterone production, which can cause testicular shrinkage, infertility, polycythemia, and accelerated hair loss in many patients. Clomiphene works differently — it blocks estrogen receptors, which signals your body to produce more testosterone naturally. Our patients routinely see an 80–100% increase in free testosterone within 30 days while preserving fertility and avoiding the common side effects of synthetic testosterone.

I'm already on TRT from another provider. Can I switch?

Yes. Dr. Michael regularly helps men transition from injectable TRT to a clomiphene-based protocol. The process requires a careful evaluation and a structured transition — message Dr. Michael directly to discuss your specific situation.

Currently The Performance Institute serves patients in Georgia only. We are a telehealth practice — all consultations are conducted by video, and medication is shipped directly to your home anywhere in Georgia.

Does The Performance Institute accept insurance?

No. We are a cash-pay practice. Our $199/month membership includes your physician consultation, medication shipped to your door, and lab oversight — with no insurance complexity or surprise bills.

How long before I notice results?

Most patients begin noticing improvements in energy, libido, and mental clarity within 4-8 weeks. Lab values showing testosterone increases are typically measurable at the first follow-up blood draw at 4-6 weeks.

I'm concerned about fertility. Is clomiphene safe?

Unlike injectable testosterone which causes infertility by suppressing sperm production, clomiphene actually stimulates your body's natural testosterone and sperm production simultaneously. In fact, clomiphene was initially developed as a treatment for male infertility and is still used for this purpose today — making it uniquely suited for men who want to optimize testosterone while preserving or improving fertility.
